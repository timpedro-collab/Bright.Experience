## CROSS-CUTTING PATTERNS

1. **Every staffing agency now claims a "real-time reporting platform" — but almost none name their metrics.** Elevate (IMPACT), Kru Live (Kru Report™), Circle (E5), Smollan (REX/DDE), Live Agency, Attack!, Air Fresh all sell dashboards. Only Elevate publishes KPI structure (tiers, catalogue, NPS/LTV/CPAC) and case numbers. "Dashboard" is the price of entry; *specific, named, machine-verifiable metrics* are still rare.
2. **The funnel is universally enquiry → scoped quote. Zero price transparency at agencies.** The only self-serve booking in the whole physical-sampling world is retail media (Tesco Sphere). Best-in-class intermediate steps: Mash's structured quote form (staff × days × hours), PopHaus's "fixed-price quote in one working day," Relish's "same-day response" + published pricing *drivers*, iMP's cost-benchmark blog (£0.44/sample street; £30–60k experiential tier; £150k+ tours). Publishing cost anatomy is a trust weapon almost nobody uses.
3. **Digital platforms win on attribution; physical operators win on theatre. Nobody owns both.** SoPost/Sampl track individual → request → opt-in → review → verified purchase (receipts, cashback, Nearest Store). Physical sampling counts distribution (iD's Lipton: 512,400 samples but only 2,712 data points = **0.5% data capture rate**). The measured-experience middle ground — a physical, crowd-stopping moment with digital-grade per-interaction data — is exactly where Bright's machines sit, and it is unoccupied.
4. **Data capture per interaction is the metric that separates tiers.** Digital platforms: 70%+ opt-in (SoPost), 46% opt-in (SMaS). Physical: single-digit percentage at best. Any physical mechanic that forces/rewards a digital touch (QR-to-play, lead-gate to dispense) collapses this gap.
5. **Stock and staffing logistics are productized by the leaders.** SoPost has a live Stock page with SKU-level notifications; iD runs a Milton Keynes reseller logistics hub; ATN runs backup-staff redundancy protocols and portal rosters; Attack! runs GPS check-in. Multi-location execution is sold as an operations *product*, not a promise.
6. **Benchmarking is emerging as a retention feature.** Elevate: "benchmark vs. previous campaigns and industry data." SoPost: "benchmark your performance against the industry's best." PortMA sells cost-per-activation-day benchmarks. Aggregated cross-campaign data is a moat that grows with volume.
7. **Retail media is absorbing sampling.** Tesco and Kroger sell sampling as a measurable media placement with closed-loop loyalty attribution and self-serve booking. This raises brand-side expectations for *all* physical trial: if Tesco can show incremental sales lift per sampling event, a festival sampling stand that reports "samples handed out" looks primitive.
8. **KPI-backwards campaign design.** Odore's intake starts from target conversion and opt-in rates; Sampl sells by objective (acquisition/launch/data). The digital platforms scope campaigns from the metric target, not the format.

## WHAT THEY DO BETTER THAN BRIGHT.EXPERIENCE

1. **Per-person, closed-loop purchase attribution (SoPost, Sampl, Tesco, Kroger).** Bright counts plays/leads/queue — activity metrics. SoPost tracks a *named individual* from request → trial → review → verified retail purchase (receipt upload, cashback, store-level data). Tesco/Kroger tie sampling to loyalty-card baskets and control-store baselines ("400% sales lift," "incrementality"). Bright currently cannot prove a play became a purchase.
2. **Post-interaction journey automation (Odore, Sampl, SoPost).** Automated review-request, drive-to-purchase, and *replenishment* emails timed to product usage; email-performance dashboards (open/bounce by email type). Bright's story ends at the 24h report; theirs continues for 90 days.
3. **Named CRM integrations (Sampl: Salesforce, HubSpot, Klaviyo, Ometria).** "Export leads" is weaker than "syncs to your Klaviyo before the box arrives."
4. **Lead quality validation (SamplMatch).** Dedupe, fraud filters, freebie-hunter screening, ICP matching — 20-30% of requests filtered. Machine lead capture at events has the same junk-data problem (repeat players, fake emails); nobody at Bright's layer validates.
5. **KPI packaging and benchmarking (Elevate IMPACT).** Tiered KPI packages (Starter/Advanced/Custom), a browsable KPI catalogue, custom benchmarks vs previous campaigns and industry data, movement/footfall zone tracking. Elevate sells measurement as a configurable product; Bright's telemetry is (as described) a fixed feature.
6. **Self-serve booking with in-flight reporting (Tesco Sphere).** Real self-serve purchase of physical sampling placements exists — Bright's quote-intake funnel is still consultative.
7. **Consumer sentiment capture at scale (Purity, Peekage, SoPost).** "80% said it tastes like dairy" (Alpro, n=253k), truthfulness-scored surveys, review generation feeding product pages. Plays/leads counts say nothing about what people *felt*; agencies capture verbatims and ratings.
8. **Staffing redundancy and accountability ops (ATN, Attack!).** Backup-staff protocols, GPS check-in, client-facing rosters and ambassador ratings. Bright's field ops (installation, hosting staff) have no visible equivalent guarantees.
9. **Published pricing anatomy (iMP, PopHaus).** iMP publishes real campaign costs (£0.44/sample; £43,608 activation with permit-fee detail). Bright's price bands are good, but these operators show *worked examples* that pre-qualify buyers.

## GAPS BRIGHT COULD FILL (the "measured sampling" story)

1. **Physical sampling is essentially unmeasured at the interaction level.** The evidence: 512,400 samples → 2,712 data points (iD/Lipton). Street teams count units handed out; nobody knows who took one, whether they engaged, or what happened next. A claw machine dispensing samples measures **every single interaction** — play started, duration, win/loss, lead captured, queue length, dwell. Bright can honestly claim near-100% interaction measurement where street sampling achieves <1% data capture. This is the headline wedge.
2. **"Cost per engaged sample" vs "cost per sample."** Industry benchmark is £0.44–0.50 per street sample — but that's cost per *distribution*, not per *engagement*. A machine sample includes 30-60s of voluntary branded dwell + optional lead. Bright can define and own a new metric: cost per *played* sample / cost per qualified lead per venue-day (mirroring PortMA's cost-per-activation-day language).
3. **Gamified dispensing beats table sampling on queue and social proof — and nobody quantifies it.** Circle's own insight ("the longer people engage, the higher purchase intent rises") is unexploited: no agency can measure dwell systematically. Bright's queue/dwell telemetry proves the mechanism agencies only assert.
4. **The digital platforms have no physical presence; the agencies have no attribution tech.** A partnership/API play: machine dispenses sample + QR that enrolls the recipient into a SoPost/Sampl-style post-trial journey (review request, nearest-store, cashback validation). Bright supplies the top-of-funnel moment digital platforms lack; their tooling completes the purchase loop Bright lacks.
5. **Retail media sampling is booked self-serve but the units are dull (tables, hot stations).** Tesco sells 800+ ambient units through Sphere. A "crowd-stopping machine" as a bookable retail-media *format* (front-of-store claw dispensing samples, measured to the play, tied to Clubcard-style uplift studies) is a whitespace product — no machine operator is in the retail-media catalogue.
6. **Staffing agencies white-label; machines could too.** Mash works through experiential agencies (Cheil, Iris, CSM). Elevate/Kru sell to agencies as well as brands. Bright's catalog + telemetry could be sold *through* sampling/staffing agencies as their measurement layer, rather than only competing with them.
7. **Lead validation for events doesn't exist.** SamplMatch-style dedupe/fraud/ICP scoring applied to machine-captured leads (dedupe repeat players, flag junk emails, score against ICP) would make Bright's "leads" number credible in a way agency clipboard counts never are.
8. **Post-event journeys are absent from experiential.** Nobody in the physical world sends the automated "you played, here's where to buy / here's 20% off / review what you tried" flow. Machines know the exact moment of trial — the perfect trigger Odore/SoPost only approximate.

## IMPLEMENTATION IDEAS FOR BRIGHT (specific)

**Marketing site**
1. **"Measured Sampling" landing page** (marketing site, alongside the catalog): lead with the data-capture contrast — "Street sampling captures data from fewer than 1 in 100 samples. Our machines measure every play." Use the iD/Lipton 512,400→2,712 pattern (anonymized as an industry stat) and the £0.44 cost-per-sample benchmark, then introduce **cost-per-engaged-sample** and **cost-per-verified-lead** as Bright's metrics. Target keyword space: "product sampling that converts," "measurable sampling."
2. **Publish pricing anatomy, not just bands** (quote/proposal pages): iMP and PopHaus show that worked examples (£43k activation with line-item splits) pre-qualify buyers and build trust. Add 2-3 anonymized "what £X buys" breakdowns (machine + branding + logistics + staffing + telemetry) to the proposal/price-band page.
3. **Copy angle from the sector's own stats:** Purity's "81% of samplers go on to purchase" and Kroger's "400% sales lift" are the numbers brands already believe about sampling. Frame machines as the *measurable* version of a channel that already works: "Sampling works. Now prove it."

**Customer portal (brands)**
4. **Named, tiered KPI catalogue (Elevate IMPACT pattern):** Package telemetry as *Starter* (plays, leads, queue, uptime — the current 12ish core numbers), *Advanced* (dwell, win-rate tuning, sample-stock burn rate, repeat-player rate), *Custom* (client-defined KPIs). Give every metric a name and a definition page. This turns telemetry from a feature into a rate card.
5. **Benchmarking module:** "Your plays/machine-day vs. category median; your lead-capture rate vs. all trade-show activations." Bright's fleet data makes this a compounding moat (Elevate and SoPost both sell this; both are loved for it).
6. **Sample-stock telemetry page (SoPost Stock-page pattern):** live remaining-prize/sample counts per machine per SKU, with low-stock notifications to the client and ops. SoPost proves clients love watching stock deplete in real time — and for multi-city tours it's operationally load-bearing.
7. **Post-play journey automation:** on lead capture, trigger a branded email/SMS flow — "here's where to buy" (nearest-stockist CTA à la SoPost Nearest Store, with get-directions CTR reported), review request, discount code with redemption tracking. Report open/click/redeem in the 24h proof-of-performance and a 30-day follow-up report. This extends Bright's story past the event day.
8. **Lead-quality scoring (SamplMatch pattern):** dedupe repeat players, validate emails, flag disposable domains, optional ICP match questions on the lead-capture screen. Report "verified leads" separately from raw leads — a number procurement can defend.
9. **CRM sync, named:** direct connectors (or documented Zapier/API recipes) for HubSpot, Salesforce, Klaviyo — market it as "leads in your CRM before the stand is packed down." (Ties to the existing integration stubs; document in docs/10-integrations.md when built.)

**Venue & organizer portals**
10. **Retail-media-style placement selling:** give venues/organizers a Sphere-lite: bookable machine slots with expected plays/leads per slot based on historical telemetry ("this atrium averages 900 plays/day"). Venues become distribution partners selling a measured format — exactly how Tesco sells sampling units.

**Internal ops**
11. **Field-ops accountability features (ATN/Attack! patterns):** installer/host GPS check-in, photo-proof of installed machine (REX pattern), backup-tech protocols surfaced to clients ("your activation is covered"). Cheap to build, differentiating in RFPs against staffing agencies.
12. **Agency channel:** a white-label/partner tier of the customer portal for experiential and staffing agencies (Mash/Kru/Purity all resell through agencies) — their brand on the report, Bright's machines and telemetry underneath.

**Positioning one-liner candidates**
- "Street sampling counts what you gave away. We measure what happened."
- "Every sample, measured to the play."
- "The first sampling channel with per-interaction attribution — no clipboard required."
