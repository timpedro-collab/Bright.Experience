# Market Research: Venues & Venue Marketplaces (UK/EU/US)

**Slice:** How venues sell space and sponsorships, planner/exhibitor ordering systems, non-space monetization, marketplace UX/commission models, and stadium/arena sponsorship placements — evaluated against Bright.Experience's marketing site, customer portal, VENUE portal, and organizer portal.

**Method:** 27 web searches/fetches (Aug 2026). Every claim below is sourced to a live page, rate card, PDF manual, or press release.

---

## ExCeL London (excel.london)

**How it sells.** Website IA is strictly persona-split: `/organiser`, `/exhibitor`, `/visitor` top-level paths. The organiser funnel (`excel.london/organiser/venue-spaces`) lets a prospect self-filter spaces by "Enquiring as" (Agency/Venue Finder, Association, Corporate, Exhibition Organiser, Public sector) **or** by event type (award ceremony, exhibition, sporting event…), then browse 13 named spaces (ICC Auditorium, Capital Suite, Event Halls, Platinum Suite…). No self-serve pricing — the funnel ends in named salespeople with direct phone numbers listed on the page (e.g., "Nick Coffey, Head of Business Development – Exhibitions").

**Sponsorship/media inventory.** ExCeL runs a dedicated Media & Branding team selling **400+ digital and print advertising sites** across the venue and wider estate (`/organiser/venue-services/media-branding`). Key mechanics:
- "Scalable bespoke packages to suit any budget"; in-house artwork creation, production, and installation.
- Organisers can either resell venue media inventory within their tenancy via their own nominated media company or route it through ExCeL's media team (media@excel.london).
- Their published Event Services Rate Card explicitly prompts organisers: *"Do you have sponsors and exhibitors who would benefit from amplifying their brand? Would your event benefit from an activation to launch a new product or create an immersive experience?"* — i.e., ExCeL actively upsells experiential activations as media inventory, which is exactly Bright's category.

**Exhibitor services.** In-house Exhibitor Services team (email/phone/live chat/service desk), plus a Vehicle Booking System for loading-bay slots. Rate card offers time-banded pricing ("secure the best value by booking early, or take advantage of last-minute options").

**Takeaway for Bright:** ExCeL treats activation space as bookable media inventory with a rate card, but there is no measurement story — nothing about plays, dwell, or leads per site. A Bright machine on an ExCeL concourse is a "media site" ExCeL already knows how to sell; Bright's telemetry is the differentiator ExCeL cannot provide.

## NEC Birmingham (thenec.co.uk)

**How it sells.** Organiser funnel (`/organisers/host-an-exhibition/`) leads with quantified reassurance: 18 halls, 32 suites, 186,000 sqm, "75% of the UK within a three-hour travel time," and a striking growth stat: *"on average new exhibitions grow by 37% in their second year with us."* Selling outcomes with data, not just square metres.

**Exhibitor e-commerce.** "Order exhibitor services" (`/exhibitors/products-and-services/`) is a genuine e-store: browse products (rigging, parking, stand build, media), order direct, one point of contact. Pricing is deadline-tiered and published: orders <28 days before build are surcharged **+20%**, on-site orders **+30%** — a self-serve urgency mechanic.

**Sponsorship.** `/exhibitors/advertising-and-sponsorship/`: the campus "welcomes over 7 million visitors every year," sold as advertising/commercial-partnership reach to both event exhibitors *and* national/regional businesses (i.e., NEC sells its own footfall year-round, decoupled from any one show).

**Takeaway:** NEC's deadline-tiered pricing and its "sell the venue's annual footfall as an audience" framing are both directly reusable in Bright's VENUE portal packages.

## Olympia London (olympia.co.uk)

**How it sells.** Mid-redevelopment repositioning: not a venue but a **destination campus** — Grand Hall + National Hall + new ICC (800-seat auditorium), a 4,000-cap AEG music venue, 1,575-seat theatre, two hotels (Hyatt Regency, citizenM), 550k sq ft of creative workspace. IA sells the estate, then the spaces.

**Sponsor-relevant detail.** The ICC page explicitly markets **business lounges for "headline sponsors, green rooms, VIP hospitality"** and West Hall for "exhibition-style sponsor activations" — sponsor activation is a named use-case in the space descriptions themselves. Nurture funnel: "Join the Olympia Organisers list" (content marketing to organisers) rather than instant quote.

**Takeaway:** Olympia writes sponsor activations into the copy of each space page. Bright's venue-facing pages should do the reverse for venues: name the exact concourse/lobby locations where a machine placement earns.

## Business Design Centre, London (businessdesigncentre.co.uk)

**How it sells.** Mid-size venue; the enquiry form (`/organisers/venue-enquiry/`) is a **3-step structured wizard**: organiser contact → event description → event details (attendees, desired start date + "date is flexible" checkbox, event sector dropdown, public/trade). Structured intake at a 6,000 sqm venue.

**Sponsorship & e-store (from their published Rules & Regulations Handbook 2025).** They maintain a **"Sponsorship and Branding Opportunities brochure"** (venue front window glazing, external poster sites, social channels) — but it's request-by-email via the Marketing & Communications Director. They also run an in-house **online shop with an organiser portal**: "The online system consists of an organiser portal, which allows organisers to keep an eye on any exhibitor's bookings via our shop" — the venue gives each event a unique shop link for the exhibitor manual, and the organiser watches exhibitor orders in real time.

**Takeaway:** Even a mid-size venue has an organiser-visible exhibitor storefront; but its sponsorship inventory is a PDF brochure behind an email. That gap (packaged, priced, self-serve sponsor placements) is exactly what Bright's VENUE portal packages/placements model addresses.

## Tobacco Dock, London (tobaccodocklondon.com)

## Broadwick (Printworks London, Drumsheds) — broadwick.com

Two-sided model: **Broadwick Live** programs its own ticketed content (1M+ tickets/yr), and **Broadwick Spaces** hires the venues to brands (case studies: Nike Innovation, Pogba x Adidas launch, Aphex Twin x Red Bull, Dua Lipa Studio 2054 livestream, Burberry fashion show at Drumsheds). **Takeaway:** Broadwick proves venues increasingly sell *audiences and cultural moments*, not floor space. Bright's venue pitch ("your machine placement earns revenue and gives sponsors a measurable audience") matches where this market is going; Broadwick venues are also prime targets for hosted machine placements between events.

## Javits Center, NYC (javitscenter.com)

**How it sells space.** "Book an Event" with floor plans, a **North Javits 360 virtual tour**, and a "Special Events Collection." Persona nav: Book / Plan / Exhibit / Attend.

**Exhibitor ordering.** New **Javits Service Portal** ("Jake") — forms-free online ordering for electrical, technology, plumbing; chatbot "Digit" for exhibitor services questions; food/beverage delivery via a separate platform (CultivatedGo). Wi-Fi network supports 130,000 devices.

**Sponsorship inventory.** Venue media is resold through show organizers: e.g., ICSC New York's sponsorship kit sells the **"JCTV" network — a 600 sq ft LED jumbotron in the Crystal Palace plus 70 plasma TVs across 4 levels** — inside $2,500–$55,000 sponsor packages, alongside hanging banners, door clings, escalator wraps. The venue holds the inventory; the organizer packages and prices it per show.

**Takeaway:** The organizer-as-reseller pattern matters for Bright's organizer portal: trade-show organizers are used to reselling venue media inventory to their sponsors — a Bright machine should be one more line in that sponsorship kit, with the unique addition of per-play/lead reporting.

## McCormick Place, Chicago (mccormickplace.com)

**Exhibitor ordering.** Online ordering portal for technology services (venue is the exclusive internet/telecom provider); event-scoped ordering ("select your event, choose services"), deadline-based advance/standard rates, on-site Technology Services desk.

**Sponsorship.** Facility-wide customizable partnership program: exterior signage, digital marquees, corporate hospitality, F&B integration, "Art Gallery and Guest Amenity Presenting Sponsorships," business-lounge entitlements — pitched as "brand affinity through direct association with the biggest convention center in North America."

**Measurement (standout).** McCormick partnered with **Scanalytics** to install **2.6M sq ft of under-floor foot-traffic sensors** — "the world's smartest convention center." Scanalytics provides exhibitors "anonymous data… to understand visitor behavior, increase engagement, and improve design effectiveness," with an **API exposing entrances, engagements, visits, and heatmaps** and real-time dashboards. Their sales pitch: "a source of truth data for exhibitors within their booth and a true ROI." MPEA's CEO justified it as making McCormick "more competitive in attracting conventions."

**Takeaway:** The biggest US venue invested millions in floor sensors to sell *measurability* as a venue amenity. Bright's machines produce richer engagement data (plays, leads, dwell, queue) natively — "measured to the play" competes directly with, and beats, passive foot-traffic analytics at the placement level.

## Las Vegas Convention Center (LVCC / LVCVA)

**Sponsorship mechanics (from the LVCC Building User's Manual and exhibitor guides).**
- Public-area advertising requires paying LVCVA **15% of the lessee's gross advertising revenue** — the venue takes a formal rev-share on sponsorship sold within its walls.
- Digital screen network across West/North/Central/South halls, including a **10,000 sq ft atrium screen**; ads sold in 8-second spots. If a show doesn't buy the digital package, LVCVA's own advertisers run on all screens during show days — inventory never sits idle.
- Content pipeline is operationalized: files due 15 business days out, $500 rush fee, managed by a dedicated contractor (Ventech Solutions).

**Self-serve sponsorship storefronts (standout).** Shows at LVCC use **Freeman's "Envision" e-commerce platform**: an actual cart/checkout where an exhibitor buys "Digital Signage Ad Spot — $4,000.00 per ad, 8-second spot, 11 screens/7 locations, limited to 4 spots per company," with file specs and due dates on the product page (example: MJBizCon storefront at envision.freeman.com). IBS 2027 sells "Convention Center Digital Ad – 8 seconds – $10,000" the same way.

**Takeaway:** Sponsorship placements at the biggest US venue are already bought like SKUs in a webshop, with unit pricing, inventory caps, and deadlines. This is the strongest single pattern for Bright's VENUE portal packages/placements and sponsorship listings: productize each machine placement as a SKU (location, dates, price band, availability, spec/creative deadline), and take the LVCVA-style rev-share as the commercial model.

## Moscone Center, San Francisco (moscone.com)

**Sponsorship mechanics.** Publishes its advertising commission schedule openly: **$500 city commission per advertiser, per advertisement, per location** for any sponsored graphic/display in lobbies/corridors; every LED screen individually enumerated (South Lobby West #20, North Concourse #17…). Sponsor advertising may not face outward from the building. Licensees must submit a floor plan of all advertising locations for operator approval. GES runs a **Moscone sponsorship storefront** (moscone.sponsorship.ges.com) listing sellable locations by code (N0-CL1, S1-ESC1, W1-B7…) — the whole building is inventoried as addressable ad units.

**Takeaway:** Both Moscone and LVCC show venues formalizing per-location, per-advertiser fees. Bright's placement model in the VENUE portal should carry a location-coded inventory register per venue (each machine slot = coded, priced, approvable unit), which also makes venue-approval workflows (floor-plan submission) explicit.

## RAI Amsterdam (rai.nl)

**Exhibitor e-commerce (most complete in EU).** A true **Exhibitor Services webshop** (Intershop-powered, service.rai.nl): stand construction, hostesses, furniture, catering, ICT, parking, cleaning — with account login, a helpdesk, and a rule that the shop closes 5 days before the fair (after which the on-site Event Desk takes over). The exhibitor journey page even publishes a "customer journey" map with a webshop and a separate Exhibitor Portal.

The webshop's Promotions & Visibility category runs discount campaigns ("20% discount — guide visitors with digital branding"). Notably, RAI's page invites novel formats: *"Looking for something that isn't listed? We are always in for new concepts and activations."*

**Takeaway:** RAI is the most productized venue-media operation found in the EU — a webshop category for visibility products with discount mechanics. But every product is impressions-based; none report engagement. RAI-style venues are ideal early adopters for a Bright machine as a premium "promo point" with actual play/lead data.

## Messe Berlin (messe-berlin.de / shop.messe-berlin.de)

**Portal stack.** Per the ITB Berlin step-by-step guide, exhibitors touch three systems: the **Exhibitor Portal** (stand registration, co-exhibitors, order confirmation, placement offers), the **BECO webshop** (tickets, parking, logistics, stand construction, services, permits), and the **Advertising Shop** (shop.messe-berlin.de) with a **Content Manager** for editing the exhibitor's own profile.

**Sponsorship productization.** Advertising portfolios publish per-item prices like a retail catalogue: Stand Logo €95, Top Listing €200, Sandwich Banner €200, Floor Graphic Indoor €250, Ultra-Stretch Screen €700, **Mobile digital Display €1,200**. Every booth automatically includes a base "Media Package," upsold via upgrades. Digital placements (app splash-screen logos, top banners "limited to 6 exhibitors per position," top listings) are sold with explicit scarcity and direct links to the buyer's profile.

**Takeaway:** Messe Berlin proves micro-priced, self-serve sponsorship works at €95–€1,200 price points with scarcity mechanics ("only 6 per position"). Bright's sponsorship placements can borrow: automatic base package on any venue partnership + priced upgrades + capped inventory per location.

## Hire Space (hirespace.com)

**Model.** UK-leading venue marketplace. Free listing tier + paid **Core (from £1,950/yr)** and **Prime (from £4.8k/yr)** listings (search prominence, category-page features, account manager, analytics). **10% commission on final booking revenue** (venue hire + F&B + AV, ex-VAT), on both direct bookings (venue self-reports final spend post-event, chased by automated emails) and "handled" bookings, where **Hire Space becomes the legal counterparty to both sides** — standardized digital contract with the client, mirrored contract with the venue, Hire Space invoices and pays out minus 10%.

**Product (from homepage, Aug 2026).** Now heavily AI-led: "**Quotes in 10 seconds**" — data-backed instant price estimates on submitting a brief, with cheaper look-alike suggestions if over budget; "Deep Research" that searches beyond its own listings ("3+ million venues," including unopened venues); a concierge tier (free for 100+ pax / £10k+ budgets); **Hire Space 360** for enterprises (£2m–£30m event programmes: one contract, one invoice, policy/compliance, 37 currencies, spend dashboards; claims 23% average spend savings). **Takeaway:** Three steals for Bright: (1) instant data-backed price estimates against a brief — Bright already has price bands, so a "quote in seconds" experience on the marketing site is achievable; (2) the handled-booking construct (platform as counterparty, standardized contracts) for machine placements; (3) the MCP/AI-assistant channel — being bookable inside AI assistants is now a live distribution channel in this industry.

## VenueScanner (venuescanner.com)

**Model.** ~23,000 venues, UK-centric. Pivoted from commission-only to **subscription tiers**: Essentials/Premium plans pay 0% commission on marketplace bookings and receive full organiser contact details; legacy "Basic" listings pay **12.5% commission**; their in-house agency ("VenueScanner for Business") bookings carry ~10%. Sells venues on "intelligent matching" (pre-qualified, high-intent enquiries) and SEO-optimised listing pages. Ranking algorithm factors disclosed in T&Cs: response rate, response speed, number of open enquiries, review scores, cancellation history — venues are ranked on operational responsiveness.

**Takeaway:** The subscription-not-commission pivot shows venues resent success fees and will pay flat SaaS for qualified demand — relevant to how Bright prices the VENUE portal. Ranking-by-responsiveness is a mechanic Bright could apply to venue partners (fast-responding venues surface higher for sponsor placements).

## HeadBox (headbox.com)

**Model.** "AI-powered" B2B venue platform (30k+ venues; claims 5,000 events delivered across 112 cities in 2025). Booker flow is a **brief-builder** (location, dates, style, budget) → AI + human curation → venues respond → booker "matches" (Tinder-style accept/decline) → direct contact. Venue side: free listing, 0% commission on direct enquiries; **10% on corporate enquiries** hand-picked by their venue-finding team; paid subscription unlocks a commission-free "Lead Feed." For enterprise: interactive shareable proposals with 3D tours, **pre-agreed terms/standardised contracts** ("no more protracted legal reviews"), monthly consolidated invoices. Publishes an **LLM-reference page** (headbox.com/inspiration/llm-info) written specifically so AI assistants describe them correctly.

**Takeaway:** The brief-first, match-based flow is closest to Bright's recommendation quiz → proposal funnel — validation that brief-led beats browse-led for corporate buyers. The LLM-info page is a cheap, clever SEO/AEO tactic Bright's marketing site should copy.

## Tagvenue (tagvenue.com)

**Model.** 20–22k venues across 6 countries, 700k+ organisers/month. Free listing; commission only on confirmed completed bookings: **10%** (invoice-based categories, US/CA/SG), **12.5%** (UK/IE/AU), **15%** (categories with mandatory online payments — meetings, production, dry hire). Listing UX strengths: **100+ filters**, upfront starting prices/per-person rates/minimum spend **based on real booking data**, response-time badges, 100k+ reviews, live lead feed for venues ("see date, budget, requirements, send an offer before other venues respond"), venue-side tooling with AI proposal templates, automated follow-ups, and 2-way calendar sync (Google/iCal/Outlook).

**Takeaway:** Real-booking-data pricing transparency is the conversion engine. Bright's public catalog already shows price bands — the upgrade is Tagvenue-style "from £X, typical spend £Y for events like yours" derived from actual quote/booking history.

## Peerspace (peerspace.com)

**Model.** Hourly-rental marketplace, 50k+ hosts. Free listing; flat **20% host service fee** on booking amount + add-ons; guests pay upfront; hosts paid within 3–7 days; **Instant Book** against confirmed calendar availability; hosts sell **add-ons** (equipment, services) through the same checkout; automated overtime billing in 30-min increments at 1x/1.5x; built-in insurance ($1M liability + $25k damage per booking) as a trust layer.

**Takeaway:** Peerspace is the fully self-serve end of the spectrum: transaction, add-ons, overtime, and insurance all productized. The add-ons-in-checkout pattern maps to Bright quote/proposal upsells (extra prize stock, branded wraps, lead-capture upgrades); the insurance-included trust framing maps to Bright's logistics/damage story for venues hosting machines.

## Cvent Supplier Network (cvent.com)

**Model.** The B2B heavyweight: ~340k venue profiles, 3M+ RFPs/year, $17B+ sourced (2024). Planners build one RFP and blast it to multiple venues, compare bids side-by-side, export to Excel; venues manage leads via a supplier portal (lead-catcher lists, proposal workflows). Monetization is **advertising, not commission**: paid **Diamond Listings (tiers 1–4)** buy search prominence, richer profiles (photos, "need dates," seasonality), and top-of-results picture ads that bypass filters. New AI layer ("CventIQ" venue recommendations) explicitly rewards rich, well-maintained profiles with more RFP flow. Also supports **instant booking for small meetings** and photorealistic floor plans.

**Takeaway:** Cvent proves the "structured RFP → comparable proposals" pattern at scale, and that suppliers will pay for placement when demand is aggregated. Bright's organizer portal could adopt need-dates/seasonality (venues flagging gap weeks where a Bright machine placement or activation would be welcome), and Bright's proposals should stay structurally comparable (like Cvent bid comparisons) rather than free-form PDFs.

## Stadiums & Arenas: The O2, Wembley, US arena operators

**The O2 (AEG).** The clearest template for what Bright's venue proposition wants to become:
- **Named, always-on branded activation zones**: the **Virgin Media Gamepad** — a permanent free gaming destination in the O2 foyer (5-year deal, brokered by AEG Global Partnerships; 3 zones: streaming studio, 16 gaming pods, Priority-members lounge; ~50,000 visitors and 125 content creators in its first 6 months, per its Campaign Experience Awards entry). A brand pays the venue to run a *permanent interactive game attraction* — functionally identical to a Bright machine placement, at flagship scale.
- Partner page directs fans to "activation zones on level 1, 4 and arena floor"; premium inventory includes The NinetyThird by Qatar Airways members' club and the Pepsi Deck branded seating; the 2026 Carlsberg Britvic renewal (10 years) bundles pouring rights + a new branded concourse bar + "brand activation opportunities across key arena events."
- AEG Global Partnerships operates as an in-house sponsorship sales/activation agency with case studies and stated KPIs (dwell time, engagement, Priority sign-ups).

**Wembley (FA).** Sells naming rights ("Wembley Stadium connected by EE"), premium hospitality ("Experiences by Wembley Stadium" membership tiers), and event-day branding via agency CSM Live, whose contract explicitly covers "creative technology… and innovations that **enable measurement of fan engagement**." Around marquee events (UCL Final 2024), sponsors (PepsiCo, Just Eat) built fan-zone activations — including a music-reactive football challenge and a crane-grab "Delivery Bag Grabber Game" (a human claw machine!) — via agencies TRO/Fuse.

**US operators.** **Oak View Group Global Partnerships**: 42 naming-rights deals and **$6.3B in partnership revenue across 629 brands since 2019**; sells venue sponsorship as a productized service (valuation, benchmarking, activation, analytics) to venues that can't staff it; Samsung partnership rolls sponsor-addressable displays across 400 venues "to drive more revenue opportunities through paid sponsorships." **Relo Metrics** launched **In-Venue Measurement** (2024): computer-vision valuation of every in-stadium placement (LEDs, scoreboards, signage) from the seated fan's perspective, unified with broadcast/social media value in one dashboard — because "properties are missing out on sponsorship exposure across millions of attendees."

**Takeaway:** Sports has already built the full stack Bright envisions for venues — dedicated sponsorship sales arms, named activation zones with engagement KPIs, and third-party measurement currencies. Exhibition venues have none of this. Bright can be "AEG Global Partnerships + Relo Metrics, in a box" for convention centers and mid-size venues.

## Reference point: machine-placement economics (route-operator model)

For the VENUE portal's revenue story, the amusement industry's standard splits are well documented: venue share of gross machine revenue runs **15–40%** depending on venue type (malls 30–40%, cinemas/FECs 25–35%, service retail 10–20%); alternative structures are flat monthly fees ($150–$400/machine), or **minimum guarantee ($300–$500/mo) + 10–18% above threshold** — the guarantee+percentage structure is "preferred by mall property managers as it de-risks their floor space commitment." Operators carry prizes, maintenance, transport; venues provide floor space, power, footfall. Contracts standardly cover audit rights, payment schedule, placement location, and termination.

**Takeaway:** Bright's venue dashboards should let venues model these exact structures (rev-share %, flat fee, guarantee+overage) because that's the mental model venue commercial teams already have — then differentiate by adding the sponsorship layer (a machine that also carries a paying brand earns 2x: play revenue + placement fee) with telemetry to prove both.

## Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Persona-split IA is universal.** Every major venue splits Organiser / Exhibitor / Visitor (ExCeL, NEC, Javits, RAI, Messe Berlin). Nobody mixes audiences on one page. Bright's marketing site should keep brand / venue / organizer journeys equally hard-separated.
2. **Exhibitor services have become e-commerce; sponsorships are following.** RAI's webshop, Messe Berlin's BECO + Advertising Shop, NEC's product store, Javits' Service Portal, McCormick's online ordering — and now Freeman Envision/GES storefronts selling sponsorship placements with carts, unit prices, per-company caps, and file deadlines. Self-serve checkout for branded placements is the industry's current frontier.
3. **Deadline-tiered pricing everywhere.** NEC (+20%/+30% late surcharges), McCormick (advance vs standard rates), LVCC ($500 rush fees). Urgency pricing is normalized; Bright quotes/proposals can use it.
4. **Venues formalize rev-share on sponsorship revenue inside their walls.** LVCVA takes 15% of gross public-area ad revenue; Moscone charges $500/advertiser/location; venue share of amusement machines is 15–40%. Venues expect a cut of any monetized placement — Bright's venue commercial model should lead with this.
5. **Everything is sold on impressions; nothing on engagement.** ExCeL's 400 sites, RAI's 850 screens, ACV's polySTAGE, LVCC's 10,000 sq ft screen — all priced on location and footfall (NEC: "7 million visitors"). Only two measurement plays exist: Scanalytics floor sensors (passive foot traffic) and Relo Metrics (sports-only, exposure valuation). **No one measures participation.**
6. **Marketplaces converge on: upfront pricing from real data, brief-first matching, response-time-ranked suppliers, and platform-as-counterparty contracts.** Commission bands cluster at 10–15% (Peerspace 20%); several (VenueScanner, HeadBox) are pivoting to SaaS subscriptions + 0% commission because venues resist success fees.
7. **AI-assistant distribution has arrived.** Hire Space ships an MCP integration inside Claude/ChatGPT; HeadBox publishes an LLM-reference page; Cvent pushes "CventIQ" AI sourcing. Discovery is moving into AI assistants.
8. **Stadium sponsorship is 10 years ahead of exhibition-venue sponsorship.** Named activation zones, multi-year branded attractions (Virgin Media Gamepad), in-house sales arms (AEG GP, OVG GP), and measurement currencies (Relo) — none of which mid-tier exhibition/event venues have.

## What they do better than Bright.Experience (as described)

- **Instant, data-backed quoting (Hire Space).** "Quotes in 10 seconds" from a brief, with automatic cheaper alternatives. Bright has price bands in proposals but (as described) no instant public estimate.
- **True self-serve checkout for placements (Freeman Envision / GES / Messe Berlin).** Real carts, SKU pricing, inventory caps, creative-file deadlines with rush fees. Bright's quote-intake → proposal flow is still human-mediated.
- **Deadline/urgency pricing (NEC, McCormick, LVCC).** Codified early-bird/late/on-site tiers. Bright proposals don't (as described) vary price by lead time.
- **Organiser-visible supplier ordering (BDC, RAI, Messe Berlin).** The organiser watches exhibitors' orders in real time in the venue shop. Bright's organizer portal could mirror this for machine/activation orders across an event.
- **Platform-as-counterparty contracting (Hire Space handled bookings; HeadBox pre-agreed terms; Peerspace payments+insurance).** Standardized contracts, consolidated invoicing, built-in insurance. Bright's venue/sponsor deals presumably still bilateral.
- **Ranking suppliers by responsiveness (VenueScanner, Tagvenue).** Response-rate/speed as an explicit algorithmic ranking factor and displayed badge.
- **Reconfigurable branded infrastructure (Austria Center polySTAGE).** The venue owns a spectacular, instantly re-skinnable sponsor canvas — a stronger "wow" than a static catalog page conveys.
- **AI-assistant presence (Hire Space MCP, HeadBox llm-info).** Bookable inside ChatGPT/Claude; machine-readable brand facts for LLMs.
- **Audience-first venue marketing (Broadwick, NEC, O2).** Selling attendance figures, second-year growth stats, and cultural cachet rather than square metres.

## Gaps Bright could fill

1. **No venue can package, price, and *measure* sponsor activations.** The entire venue-media stack (ExCeL's 400 sites, RAI's 850 screens, LVCC's jumbotrons) reports zero engagement data. Scanalytics measures anonymous footsteps; Relo values *exposure* in sports only. A Bright machine reporting plays, leads, dwell, and queue per placement is the only *participation-measured* sponsor unit in the venue market. "Measured to the play" has no incumbent competitor in this segment.
2. **Mid-size venues have sponsorship brochures, not sponsorship systems.** BDC's inventory is a PDF requested by email; Tobacco Dock and Broadwick sell brandability with no productized sponsor units at all. There is a whitespace product: a white-label "sponsorship shop" for venues — which is essentially Bright's VENUE portal packages + embeddable widgets.
3. **No marketplace covers sponsor placements *inside* venues.** Hire Space/Tagvenue/HeadBox/Peerspace transact space hire; Cvent transacts RFPs; Envision/GES storefronts are per-show and organizer-controlled. Nobody operates a cross-venue marketplace where a *brand* books a measurable activation placement across a network of venues. Bright's venue network + machines + telemetry is exactly that supply.
4. **Venues can't monetize dark days.** Cvent invented "need dates" for hotels; exhibition venues have gap weeks between tenancies with zero footfall products. A hosted Bright machine (in a venue's public realm, food hall, or co-located destination — NEC/Resorts World, Olympia's hotels/theatre, O2's entertainment district) earns during dark days and between events.
5. **Organizers resell venue media manually.** ICSC hand-builds Javits signage into $2.5k–$55k sponsor decks. An organizer portal that auto-composes sponsorship kits (including machine placements with projected plays/leads from historical telemetry) would beat the PDF-prospectus status quo.
6. **The amusement route-operator model is analog.** Venue splits (15–40%) are negotiated on paper with coin-box audits. Bright can be the first to give venues a real-time revenue dashboard per machine — trust through telemetry, matching the audit-rights clause venues already demand.

## Implementation ideas for Bright (specific)

**Marketing site**
1. **Instant estimate on the quiz/quote intake** (Hire Space "quotes in 10 seconds"): after the recommendation quiz, show a price band immediately from historical quote data, with a cheaper alternative machine suggested if over budget — before human follow-up.
2. **Publish an `llm-info` page** (HeadBox pattern): a machine-readable page stating what Bright.Experience is, machine types, geographies, price-band logic, and how briefs work, so AI assistants answer correctly. Longer-term: an MCP server exposing catalog search + quote intake (Hire Space precedent).
3. **Case-study pages sell audience numbers** (Broadwick pattern): lead each case study with plays/leads/dwell stats, not photos.

**VENUE portal**
4. **Placement-as-SKU inventory register** (LVCC/Moscone/Envision pattern): each machine slot at a venue = a coded unit (location, footfall estimate, dates, price band, availability, creative deadline, per-sponsor cap). Venue "Packages" page composes SKUs into tiers; add scarcity ("2 concourse slots remaining for Q4"). Include an approval step mirroring Moscone's floor-plan sign-off.
5. **Revenue-model configurator on the venue dashboard**: let venues pick rev-share % / flat fee / minimum-guarantee+overage (route-operator structures they already know), and show live earnings per machine from telemetry — the "coin-box audit, digitized."
6. **Dark-day monetization view**: calendar showing venue gap weeks (Cvent "need dates" pattern) where a hosted machine or sponsor activation could run; one-click "make this week available for sponsorship."
7. **Embeddable widgets v2** (Skedda/Evently/Tagvenue patterns): beyond content embeds, ship an enquiry-to-estimate widget ("Host a Bright machine — see what your venue could earn") that runs the earnings model inline on the venue's own site, HTTPS-only iframe.
8. **Venue responsiveness scoring** (VenueScanner/Tagvenue pattern): rank venue partners in sponsor-facing search by response speed/rate; show badges.

**Organizer portal**
9. **Auto-generated sponsorship kit builder** (Javits/ICSC + Envision pattern): organizers compose sponsor packages that bundle Bright machine placements with projected plays/leads (from telemetry history at comparable events), export as a shareable storefront rather than a PDF — with tiered pricing (Gold/Silver caps, like ICSC's max-6-sponsors tiers) and deadline-based pricing (NEC's +20%/+30% late-order mechanic).
10. **Organizer-visible order dashboard** (BDC/RAI pattern): organizers see all machine/activation orders by their exhibitors/sponsors for their event in real time.

**Customer (brand) portal**
11. **Add-ons in checkout** (Peerspace pattern): prize restock, branded wrap, lead-capture upgrade, extra event days as one-click additions on an accepted proposal; automated overtime/extension billing.
12. **Benchmarked proof-of-performance** (Relo Metrics pattern): post-event reports should benchmark each placement against category norms ("your claw machine ranked in the top 20% of retail activations for plays/hour") — turning Bright's telemetry corpus into a valuation currency the way Relo did for sports exposure.

**Commercial/positioning**
13. **Pitch venues as "AEG Global Partnerships in a box"**: mid-size venues (BDC, Tobacco Dock, regional convention centers) can't staff a sponsorship sales arm; Bright's VENUE portal + machine network + measurement is that capability, productized. Use the O2 Virgin Media Gamepad (permanent branded game attraction, 50k visitors/6 months) as the flagship analogy in venue sales decks.
14. **Adopt the venue rev-share norm proactively**: offer venues LVCVA-style participation (e.g., 15% of sponsorship gross on placements in their building) in standard terms — it matches their existing mental model and removes a negotiation.
