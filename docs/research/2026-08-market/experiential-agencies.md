# Experiential / Event Agencies — Client-Service & Measurement Models

**Research slice:** experiential and event agencies (UK/EU/US) — client journeys, measurement frameworks, client-facing portals, services-page IA, and productization — as competitive/ecosystem context for Bright.Experience.
**Method:** ~29 web searches/fetches, August 2026. Focus on systems, flows, service models and measurement (visual/homepage design was covered in a previous round).
**Coverage:** 24 organizations (14+ required): Freeman, GES (+Visit by GES, Spiro), Momentum Worldwide, Jack Morton, George P. Johnson, Imagination, TRO, Amplify, Sense, MKTG/Dentsu, Wasserman, NVE, Superfly/OGX, Set Creative, 2Heads, Live Union, Identity, Inspira, Mosaic, BeCore, Kreate, PrettyGreen, Cake/Havas Play, Factory 360.

## Freeman (US, global) — the deepest productized data + portal stack in the industry

**Named products (all verified):**
- **Fuzion by Freeman™** (fuzion.freeman.com) — an open integration ecosystem that connects event data across registration, mobile apps, chatbots, floor-planning, virtual platforms and analytics visualization. Positioned as "the integration ecosystem for event data & content." Launched 2017.
- **Quant by Freeman™** — "decision-based metrics via data visualization in one, real-time, role-based dashboard." Part of the 2019 "Freeman Data Solutions" five-product suite (also includes Data Analysis, formerly ESI, with "50+ years of industry benchmarks").
- **XLNC framework** — Freeman's exhibitor measurement framework (documented in their "Exhibit measurement for today's events" PDF): defines outcomes per attendee goal (eXperience, Learning, Networking, Commerce) and maps in-person vs virtual metrics to each (e.g., meetings held, leads generated → sales, content downloads). Explicitly advises a measurement plan per show and per medium, with portfolio benchmarks across shows.
- **Exhibitor Services Platform (ESP)** — brand-new (announced Dec 2025 at IAEE Expo! Expo!, rolling out early 2026). "The end of fragmented exhibitor portals. One seamless journey from order to load-out." Features: streamlined event home page, interactive checklists tailored per booth, booth-based shopping, digitized Material Handling Agreements (self-service from phone), real-time visibility into shipping/handling/invoice status, integrated digital care tools. Freeman's exec framing: "It's all about giving them time back."
- Freeman publicly names **five proprietary platforms**: Blue Echo (spatial intelligence), ESP (exhibitor journey), EnvisionPro (sponsorship management), Passport (order fulfillment), Orchestrate (presentation content). Plus an AI-powered sponsorship marketplace (with Clarity Media Partners).

**Client journey:** freeman.com routes via "Let's chat" and a newsletter-gated enquiry; the actual operational journey happens in ESP (checklists → orders → tracking). Freeman explicitly modeled ESP "after top consumer brand experiences" and pilots it with NPS-style post-order surveys.

**Why it matters for Bright:** Freeman is the strongest proof that a checklist-driven, single-pane portal with real-time order/logistics status is now the expected standard for event services. But Freeman's platforms serve *organizers and exhibitors of trade shows*, not brand activations — the checklist + telemetry combination for a *branded activation* is still open territory.

## Jack Morton (IPG, US/UK/global) — "ROX" and the ROI/KPI/Insights three-tier

- Measurement page: "**It's not just about ROI — it's ROX (Return on Experience).** We go beyond surface-level metrics… connects attention, engagement, and behavior—capturing how experiences drive social amplification, shift perception, and influence what people do next."
- Published methodology (Ben Grossman, SVP Strategy): a **three-tier reporting hierarchy** —
  1. **ROI** for the C-suite: cohort analysis of attendee behavior pre/post event vs a control group; cost-per-acquisition, retention, pipeline value, advocacy, cost avoidance.
  2. **KPIs** for the VP level: attendance, conversion rates, NPS, brand/message familiarity, satisfaction, attendee quality, social sentiment.
  3. **Insights** for the working team: venue, timing, flow, quality — explicitly "shouldn't be part of the metrics reported to the VP or C-levels."
- Sells an "Event Portfolio Optimization" report with ROI/ROE/ROO frameworks and dashboards as a lead magnet.
- Homepage story: "strategy, creative, production, innovation and measurement — in one seamless global offering… experience becomes a growth engine." Trend piece literally titled "If You Can't Measure It, You Can't Maximize It."
- Client journey: "Let's Chat" → generic contact form. No pricing, no scoping transparency.

**Why it matters:** the ROI/KPI/Insights **audience-tiered reporting** is the single most stealable idea for Bright's proof-of-performance reports: one report, three layers (CMO summary / marketing-manager KPIs / ops learnings).

## Imagination (independent, London/global) — XPKit and "engaged minutes"

- **XPKit** — "our live experience platform," used to measure "brand engagement and behavioural results from hundreds of experiences over the past ten years." Aggregates data across physical and virtual touchpoints. This is the closest thing among agencies to Bright's telemetry platform — an actual software product for experience data capture.
- **Depth & Reach framework** — deliberately maps experiential to the Binet/Field long-term (reputation) vs short-term (conversion) marketing-effectiveness language so CMOs can compare experiences "like-for-like with all other activities in the marketing mix."
- Signature metric: **engaged minutes** — "can be applied to every type of experience, in every market and sector, and can be compared to other brand KPIs such as reach, website visitors and video views." Then NPS/brand-funnel as the second tier; single unified ROI framework as the third.
- Tracked metrics quoted: guest count, dwell time, social shares, content views, lead conversion, sales conversion.
- Services IA: five plain-English offers (Consultancy, Brand destinations, Content, Live events, Investor communications) — notable for including *destinations* (permanent placemaking, e.g., Guinness Open Gate Brewery London) as a product line.

**Why it matters:** "Engaged minutes" is a brilliantly simple currency. Bright's telemetry (plays, dwell, queue) can be denominated the same way: "X thousand engaged minutes, measured to the play."

## Amplify (independent, B Corp, London/LA/NY/Paris/Sydney) — the four-pillar evaluation model

- Measurement (from their Amazon pitch page, unusually candid): "We collaborate with clients from the very beginning of a project to establish the most effective measurement processes, integrating seamlessly with their existing systems." Evaluation structured on **four pillars**:

- Client journey: no structured intake, no packages; case-study-led selling (e.g., PS5 launch: "27 events in 25 countries in 24 hours").

**Why it matters:** the four-pillar model is the cleanest *taxonomy* for an activation report. Bright's proof-of-performance could adopt Brand/Experience/Amplification/Impact sections, with Experience auto-filled by machine telemetry — something Amplify has to assemble manually.

## Sense (independent, London + NY) — EMR framework and verified sales attribution

- **EMR (Experiential Measurement & Research / "Experience Measurement Reporting")** — proprietary framework launched 2014, "built on insights from over 100 deep evaluations." Key mechanic: **objectives and likely ROI are set upfront at planning stage** ("an upfront account of likely ROI and incremental profit set to be achieved"), then post-campaign reporting shows hard/soft objectives met with under/over-performance analysis. Their MD's line: recaps and anecdotal praise are "reporting, not evaluation."
- **Sense × Crisp/SKUtrak (2025, exclusive)** — industry-first: links out-of-home sampling directly to **verified EPOS sales data across the UK's five biggest grocery retailers**. "Where and when sales increased, and by how much." Kills the coupon-redemption proxy problem.
- Sales claims on services page: senior-led teams ("you work directly with experienced people, not account managers"), 65+ effectiveness awards; tracks "behavioural change, brand perception, competitor benchmarks and financial ROI."
- ~35-50 staff — proof a small agency can out-measure giants by *partnering* for ground truth data instead of building it.

**Why it matters:** the two most credible measurement moves in the whole study are (a) predicted-ROI-before-you-sign (EMR) and (b) third-party-verified outcomes (SKUtrak). Bright's price bands + telemetry make (a) trivially automatable; (b) suggests partnering for lead-quality verification (CRM sync, post-event SQL rates).

## Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Everyone now claims measurement; almost nobody productizes it client-side.** Every agency in this study leads with "measurable/measured/proven." But delivery splits into three maturity tiers:
   - *Tier 1 — named platforms clients can conceptually buy:* Freeman (Fuzion/Quant/ESP), GES (Expresso/Visit), Imagination (XPKit), Momentum (MomentumBi), Kreate (Konnected™), PrettyGreen (Ai Fame Tracker™).
   - *Tier 2 — named frameworks operated by the agency:* Jack Morton (ROX + 3-tier), GPJ (Experience Impact Score, SEM®), Sense (EMR), Amplify (4 pillars), Spiro (Brand Gravity/EMIR), Factory 360 (3 pillars), Inspira (EQ+IQ), TRO (Experience Impact).
   - *Tier 3 — "we report rigorously" with no artifact:* BeCore, Set Creative, Mosaic, Wasserman (custom builds), Live Union, most mid-tier.
   Even Tier 1 platforms are mostly *organizer/exhibitor logistics* (Freeman/GES) or *agency-internal BI* (MomentumBi). **No agency shows a brand-facing live activation dashboard with plays/leads/queue telemetry.**
2. **The client journey is uniformly unstructured.** Across 24 organizations: generic contact forms, "Let's chat," newbiz@ emails (GPJ literally says "shoot us an email"). Nobody offers: structured brief intake, instant scoping, price bands, packages, or timelines on the marketing site. The only exceptions are outside the agency tier: game suppliers (Peek & Poke: "From £795 +VAT" DIY / "From £5,000" tailored) and turnkey vehicle rentals ($10-12k/week published). Pricing transparency in experiential is so rare that third-party cost guides (Barnastics, American Guerrilla Marketing) rank for the queries agencies won't answer.
3. **Measurement is set upfront or it isn't believed.** The most credible players all moved measurement *before* the contract: Sense's EMR sets "likely ROI and incremental profit" at planning; Identity "builds measurement frameworks into every brief… before a venue is chosen"; 2Heads engineers KPIs before design; Amplify integrates measurement "from the very beginning"; NVE pre-plans success metrics. The category consensus: recap decks after the fact = "reporting, not evaluation" (Sense).
4. **Reporting is tiered by audience.** Jack Morton's ROI (C-suite) / KPI (VP) / Insights (team) hierarchy is the canonical model; GPJ frames "CMO, CFO and yes, the CEO."
5. **Third-party/ground-truth data beats self-reported data.** Sense×SKUtrak (verified EPOS sales), TRO (camera emotion analytics), Inspira (physiological wearables), GES Visit (NFC behavioral capture) — the frontier is *instrumented* proof, not surveys.
6. **Naming and trademarks manufacture IP.** ROX, Brand Gravity™, Konnected™, Strategic Experience Mapping®, XLNC, EMR, XPKit, Experience Impact Score, Total Brand Experience, Ai Fame Tracker™. A named framework is table stakes for enterprise credibility.
7. **Consolidation churn:** Cake→Havas Play, 2Heads→Spiro/GES, TRO→Omnicom Experiential Group, BeCore→Underdog, Superfly→Range, MKTG→dentsu Sports. Independents (Amplify, Imagination, Sense, NVE, Inspira) increasingly market independence itself.
8. **Sustainability reporting is becoming a fourth measurement pillar** (Amplify's Impact pillar, Live Union/ISLA Trace, Identity's ISO 20121 + PAS 2060) — mostly UK/EU-driven.
9. **Modular/reusable assets are the emerging economics story** (2Heads "Invest Once, Reuse a Hundred Times"; OGX's touring IP; turnkey roadshow assets) — the category is drifting toward Bright's model without saying so.

## What they do better than Bright.Experience (as described)

1. **Audience-tiered ROI storytelling (Jack Morton, GPJ).** Bright's 24h proof-of-performance reports plays/leads/queue — operational KPIs. The big agencies wrap equivalent data in a C-suite narrative: cohort comparisons, pipeline value, cost-per-lead vs alternatives, control-group framing. They speak CFO; a telemetry report speaks ops.
2. **Predicted ROI before signature (Sense EMR).** Sense quotes *expected* ROI and incremental profit at proposal stage. Bright's proposals show price bands but (as described) not predicted performance bands ("expect 800–1,200 plays, 250–400 leads at this venue class") — Bright has the historical telemetry to do this better than anyone and apparently doesn't surface it at proposal time.
3. **Effectiveness benchmarking depth (Freeman).** Freeman's Data Analysis arm markets "50+ years of industry benchmarks"; XLNC frames portfolio-level comparison across shows. Cross-event, cross-venue benchmark context ("your machine outperformed the venue median by 40%") is a maturity layer beyond single-event stats.
4. **Strategy/consulting altitude (GPJ, Imagination, NVE).** Agencies enter at "what should your annual experiential portfolio be," landing multi-event retainers; a catalog entry point can anchor Bright as a per-event vendor.
5. **Amplification measurement (Amplify, PrettyGreen).** They measure the *content halo* — earned SOV, social reach, share-of-search — around the live moment. Machine telemetry stops at the machine; the social/UGC layer of a crowd-stopping queue is currently unmeasured value.
6. **Category authority via research (Inspira, Imagination, Cake, Freeman).** Annual proprietary reports (ungated!) that get cited, build SEO/AI-answer presence, and open enterprise doors.
7. **Named, trademarked IP.** Everyone else has ROX/Brand Gravity™/EMR; "live telemetry" and "proof-of-performance" are generic until named.
8. **Sustainability accounting (Identity, Amplify, Live Union).** ISO 20121, carbon-per-activation, waste diversion — increasingly a procurement checkbox in UK/EU enterprise RFPs that a hardware-touring business will be asked about (and reusable machines are actually a great answer).

## Gaps Bright could fill (agencies are bespoke and slow; Bright is productized and measured)

1. **Instant, structured scoping.** No agency lets a brand go from need → scoped, priced proposal without a sales cycle. Bright's quiz → quote intake → proposal with price bands is *unique in the entire category* — nobody else publishes prices, timelines, or packages. This should be attacked head-on, not soft-pedaled.
2. **A brand-facing live dashboard.** Freeman/GES portals do logistics; MomentumBi is internal; Wasserman hand-builds dashboards per client as billable work. **No competitor offers a standing client portal showing live plays/leads/queue during an activation.** Bright's Live Event Mode is a genuine first — market it like Freeman markets ESP ("no portals, no surprises on show day" → "no decks, no waiting, no surprises").
3. **The 24-hour report as an SLA.** Agencies deliver recap decks in 2–6 weeks. A *guaranteed* 24h proof-of-performance is a service-level claim nobody in this study makes. Publish it as a promise with a compliance stat (Kreate-style "99.85%").
4. **Repeatable-unit economics for brand activations.** OGX proved productized experiences at consumer scale; 2Heads *talks about* reuse; nobody sells B2B activations as catalog products with per-event pricing and accumulated performance history per product ("this claw machine format has averaged X leads/day across 40 shows"). Bright's catalog + telemetry history = performance-backed product pages, which no bespoke agency can ever have.
5. **Fixed-price certainty.** Third-party cost guides exist *because* agencies won't publish numbers. Price bands on proposals already beat the market; publishing indicative bands on the public catalog would rank for every "activation cost" query and pre-qualify leads.
6. **Approval velocity.** Agency creative approvals run through email/decks (the tooling market — Statusbrew/Apaya/Pastel — exists because agencies lack this). Bright's versioned creative approvals with annotations in-portal is ahead of every agency studied; the gap to exploit is *speed messaging* ("brief Monday, approve artwork Wednesday, live in 3 weeks").
7. **Venue/organizer network effects.** GES Visit sells intelligence to organizers AND exhibitors. Bright's venue + organizer portals mirror this two-sided structure; no experiential agency has anything comparable (they rent venues; they don't platform them).

## Implementation ideas for Bright (specific)

1. **Name the measurement system.** Trademark-style name for the telemetry+reporting stack (e.g., "PlayProof™" / "Measured to the Play™" as the framework name). Apply it consistently on the marketing site's measurement page, proposal PDFs, and the portal header. (Pattern: ROX, Brand Gravity™, EMR, Konnected™.)
2. **Three-audience proof-of-performance report** (portal, 24h report template): restructure as (a) Executive summary — cost-per-lead, cost-per-engaged-minute, vs venue/category benchmark (CMO layer); (b) KPI detail — plays, uniques, leads, queue, dwell, conversion (marketing-manager layer); (c) Ops learnings — peak windows, prize economics, staffing notes (team layer). Straight lift from Jack Morton's ROI/KPI/Insights.
3. **Predicted performance bands in proposals** (proposal generator): alongside price bands, auto-insert historical telemetry ranges for that machine × venue type × footfall class: "Machines of this format at 3-day UK trade shows: 900–1,400 plays, 280–450 opted-in leads." This is Sense's EMR upfront-ROI move, automated — no agency can match it because none has structured cross-event data.
4. **Adopt "engaged minutes" as a headline currency** (marketing site + reports): Imagination's metric lets CMOs compare an activation to media buys. Bright's play counts × average session length computes it automatically: "412,000 engaged brand minutes this quarter."
5. **Add an Amplification section to reports** (report pipeline, roadmap): hashtag/UGC capture, QR-driven social shares from the machine screen, photo-moment counts — closes the gap vs Amplify/PrettyGreen's earned-media pillar without becoming a PR agency.
6. **Publish operational SLA stats on the marketing site** (homepage/trust bar): machine uptime %, on-time install %, % of reports delivered <24h. (Pattern: Kreate's 99.85% shift compliance; Identity's "track record, not promise.")
7. **Public FAQ page answering cost/ROI/logistics in plain language** (marketing site): copy Identity's FAQ format — "What does a branded game machine activation cost?", "How do you measure ROI?", "How fast can we be live?" This wins the search/AI-answer queries that third-party cost guides currently own, and PrettyGreen's Ai Fame Tracker shows brands are starting to audit AI-recommendation presence.
8. **Two-door intake** (quote intake flow): TRO-style split — "Pick from the catalog" (productized, fast, price-banded) vs "Brief us something bespoke" (custom builds) — so productization never reads as a ceiling for enterprise buyers.
9. **Performance-backed catalog pages** (catalog): per machine format, show anonymized lifetime stats (events run, avg plays/day, avg lead-capture rate, top verticals). No bespoke agency can ever publish this; it converts the catalog from a brochure into evidence. (Pattern: OGX's IP lifecycle logic + Freeman benchmarks.)
10. **Benchmark layer in telemetry portal** (customer portal, post-MVP): "vs your last event" and "vs venue-class median" deltas on every stat — Freeman's Data Analysis benchmarking, computed from Bright's own accumulating dataset.
11. **Sustainability one-pager per activation** (proposals + reports, lightweight): machines are reusable assets — quantify it ("this machine is on its 23rd deployment; est. X kg fabrication waste avoided vs single-use stand build"), referencing 2Heads' "invest once, reuse" framing and UK/EU procurement expectations (ISO 20121 familiarity).
12. **Annual "State of Play" research report** (marketing, authority play): aggregate anonymized telemetry across all activations — plays by venue type, queue tolerances, lead-conversion norms — published ungated (Inspira's "no forms, no friction"). Bright may hold the only structured dataset of branded-game engagement in the market; that's citable, PR-able category authority no agency can replicate.
