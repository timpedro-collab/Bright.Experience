# Event Tech Platforms & Reseller/Partner Enablement — Deep Research for Bright.Experience

Researched Aug 2026. Scope: (A) how event-technology platforms present and operate multi-sided ecosystems (organizer + exhibitor + attendee + venue), and (B) reseller/partner-enablement patterns that make it effortless for a trade-show organizer's sales team to sell Bright machines as sponsorship inventory. ~25 web searches/fetches; all claims sourced to vendor pages, product docs, or procurement data.

# HALF A — EVENT TECHNOLOGY PLATFORMS

## Cvent (Exhibitor Management + Supplier Network)

**URLs:** cvent.com/en/event-marketing-management/exhibitor-management, cvent.com/en/event-marketing-management/cvent-supplier-network, release.cvent.com (New Exhibitor Management Experience)

**Multi-sided architecture.** Cvent runs two distinct sides that map directly onto Bright's world:

1. **Exhibitor Management** — organizer-facing module with: unified exhibitor list (sortable columns, filters by status/category/**sponsorship level**), Exhibitor Portal (self-service: profile content, tasks, booth staff registration, invoices), task management with deadline tracking, sponsored sessions ("Sponsored by" badging in attendee hub), LeadCapture licenses allocated by the organizer to exhibitors, and **exhibitor-visible engagement metrics** (organizer chooses whether exhibitors can see their own engagement data — a permission toggle).
2. **Cvent Supplier Network (CSN)** — a free two-sided marketplace: ~340K venues listed, planners send RFPs, venues respond with proposals. Venues list free but **pay for increased exposure** (Planner Navigator prospecting tool, enhanced listings, ad placements). Venue profiles are self-managed (photos, capacities, rates, date-pattern preferences) and planners filter on structured fields.

**Why the design works:** the organizer stays the hub; exhibitors get *just enough* self-service (content, tasks, staff, payments) so the organizer's team isn't a bottleneck; and the "prove ROI so they rebook" loop is explicit in Cvent's own marketing copy: "Ensure they're getting value from your event – and that you're able to prove it – so that they keep coming back each year."

**Pricing model:** unpublished, layered — annual platform licence ($20K–$79K+), **per-registrant fees ~$7–12/attendee/event**, implementation $5K–$50K (eventcloud.io teardown; Vendr median annual ~$19.5K, range $5.4K–$695K). The per-registrant layer is widely resented — a lesson for Bright: avoid metered fees that punish success.

**Onboarding flow (exhibitor):** organizer imports/creates exhibitor list → assigns sponsorship level → exhibitor receives portal invite → completes profile + tasks from a checklist → registers booth staff → gets lead-capture licenses → sees engagement metrics post-event.

## Bizzabo ("Event Experience OS")

**URLs:** bizzabo.com, bizzabo.com/product, bizzabo.com/solutions/user-industry-conference-software

**Homepage story:** a single "operating system" framing — hero lists modular capabilities as cards (Registration, Website/Agenda, Mobile App, Networking, Email, **Sponsor/Exhibitor/Speaker Management**, Broadcasting, Klik SmartBadges). Three pillars: run the portfolio on one platform / reusable templates ("launch events in hours") / revenue-linked insight ("from fragmented activity to revenue-linked insight," 2,500+ CRM integrations).

**Standout hardware+data story: Klik SmartBadges™** — wearable badges enabling tap-to-connect networking, one-tap lead capture at booths, and **live heatmaps of attendee foot traffic**. This is the closest analogue to Bright's machine telemetry: physical interactions made measurable, sold to sponsors as proof. Bizzabo explicitly sells "real-time dashboards for sponsor engagement tracking" and case-study proof ("CMP boosted exhibitor leads by 315%").

**Pricing:** published floor — $499/user/mo annual, 3-user minimum = **$17,999/yr with unlimited events/registrations** (flat-rate positioning vs Cvent's metered model). Klik hardware and onsite services quoted separately.

**Why it works:** "OS" framing lets a multi-module company tell one story; the wearable makes invisible engagement tangible; flat pricing is a differentiation weapon against Cvent.

## Swapcard ("Revenue-first event platform")

**URLs:** swapcard.com, swapcard.com/platform/overview, swapcard.com/features/exhibitor-sponsor-tools, swapcard.com/features/event-monetization

**Homepage IA (fetched in full):** headline "From fragmented tools to a single revenue system," then **seven named modules** as cards: Registration, Attendee Engagement, Sessions & Speaker Portal, Exhibitor Management Portal, **Exhibitor Marketplace**, **Exhibitor Lead Center**, Analytics & Dashboards. Then role-based sections ("built for every role": VP Events, **Exhibitor/Sponsorship Director**, Hosted Buyer Program Manager, Registration Manager…). Then event-type sections with hard numbers: Trade Shows — "AI-generated leads (5x more qualified), **Exhibitor marketplace (€180K avg revenue)**, Hosted buyer programs (2–3x pricing)."

**Killer features for Bright to study:**
- **Exhibitor Marketplace** — a self-serve store *inside* the exhibitor portal where exhibitors browse and instantly purchase digital upgrades (visibility, lead tools, premium placements) "based on real demand and intent." Swapcard claims **15–30% ARPE (average revenue per exhibitor) increase** from it. This is the "organizer sells more inventory with zero sales effort" pattern.
- **Exhibitor Lead Center** — unified lead dashboard: badge scans + profile views + chats + meetings all become leads, AI-scored, CRM-synced (Salesforce/HubSpot).
- Sponsor ad placements with **impressions/clicks reporting to prove ROI** and tiered packages.

**Company story (swapcard.com/company-information):** founded on the insight that event data lives in silos so "organizers struggled to connect attendee behavior to exhibitor outcomes, and exhibitors struggled to measure ROI" — the exact connective-tissue narrative Bright needs, told as a data-layer story. Case-study proof format: "HLTH: 91% active usage vs 35% industry avg; +18% YoY leads (29,873 captured)."

## Grip (AI matchmaking + Grip Pulse analytics)

**URLs:** grip.events/products/event-matchmaking, grip.events/products/lead-generation, grip.events/products/pulse-event-data-insights-dashboards, discover.grip.events "ROI Report Bundle" PDF

**Positioning:** enterprise AI matchmaking for 5,000+ attendee shows (SXSW, RX, Messe Frankfurt, Clarion, Hyve). 16 ML algorithms, 70M+ recommendations/year.

**The gold for Bright is Grip Pulse: Exhibitor ROI** — an analytics product organizers buy to arm *their own sales teams*:
- 100+ graph real-time dashboard, 400+ data points per participant.
- **"ROI Predictor"** — combines engagement data with what the exhibitor paid + typical deal size to *predict the ROI a sponsor will generate before/during the event*. This is exactly Bright's "expected-performance data on sponsor pitch links" concept, productized.
- Benchmarking: compare an exhibitor against anonymized industry benchmarks and against its own past editions ("predict, track, benchmark and evaluate").
- Explicit sales-team use case in their copy: "Give your sales team real-time insight into the results of an exhibiting company… **Use this insight to rebook, upsell** and deliver suggestions the same day." And: "Seeing your sales team anxiously walking up to exhibitors without insight into how they are doing at the show" is the pain framing.
- Grip also has a public **revenue calculator** for pre-scheduled meeting programs ("see how much extra revenue you could add") — a calculator as an organizer-acquisition tool.

Sponsorship/digital-ad opportunities are *embedded inside the space application flow* — upsell at the moment of maximum commitment.

## A2Z Events (Personify) — Event Sales Engine

**URLs:** personifycorp.com/products/a2z-events, mya2zevents.com/solutions/event-sales-engine, PR Newswire launch release (Oct 2025)

**The most complete "sell sponsorships like e-commerce" implementation found:**
- **Sponsorship Package Gallery** — sponsors browse visually rich listings "in a familiar online-store format," filter by price and availability, see what's left (scarcity), add to cart.
- **Single cart across booths + sponsorships + add-ons**, one checkout, automated contracts and invoicing; then instant access to the Exhibitor Hub to build their profile. Their narrative: "browse an interactive floor plan with live pricing, select a booth, add two sponsorships from the Sponsorship Gallery, review everything in a single cart and check out in minutes."
- **Sponsorship Link Builder** — organizers share **direct links that drop specific sponsorship items straight into a company's cart**, and can grant early access to packages before public release. This is the closest existing analogue to Bright's "sponsor pitch links": a salesperson mid-conversation sends a link that pre-loads the deal.
- MyAccount financial hub; flexible sales modes (self-serve booth pick, tiered-priority applications, or consultative selling) — one system supporting three sales motions.
- Business Intelligence engine: cross-event, year-over-year revenue reporting.

## Zenus AI (ethical facial analysis — direct measurement comparable)

**URLs:** zenus.ai (fetched), zenus.ai/analytics, EXHIBITORLIVE partnership PR, Shepard Solutions Network PR

**What they measure:** smart IoT cameras process video locally (no video recorded/stored, no PII) and output aggregate **impressions, dwell time, demographics (age/sex), and "positive sentiment"/energy** at 95% claimed accuracy, on a live dashboard **or API** ("build your own interactive application"). Setup "<12 minutes."

**How they sell it (three-audience homepage, exactly Bright's problem):** the homepage splits into Retail Brands ("Understand your consumers"), **Event Organizers ("Attract more exhibitors and sponsors with undisputable data")**, and Exhibitors ("Maximize your ROI"). Hero case study: **"How an organizer achieved 16/16 booth renewals"** via "Return-On-Engagement reports." Their Shepard partnership copy is a blueprint: reports covering "average attendee engagement time, energy analysis, brand interest levels… allow organizers to **benchmark sponsorship engagement across all sponsors**… sponsorship sales personnel can **tailor discussions based on data-driven insights**."
- EXHIBITORLIVE 2026 partnership: show organizer buys Zenus for the whole floor and gives every exhibitor aisle-traffic/booth-visit/dwell/engagement analytics as a show amenity — measurement as sponsorship inventory.
- A/B testing framing: "examine the effectiveness of different activations and content."

**Relevance to Bright:** Zenus proves there's a market for *paid, third-party, privacy-safe engagement measurement* at events — but Zenus only measures passively. Bright's machines *generate* the engagement AND measure it (plays, leads, queue), a structurally stronger claim ("Measured to the play" beats inferred sentiment).

## RainFocus

**URLs:** rainfocus.com, spendhound.com/marketplace/rainfocus-pricing

**Positioning:** enterprise "event marketing platform" for Fortune-1000 portfolios (Oracle CloudWorld, Cisco Live, VMworld). Core concept: **Global Attendee Profile** — one persistent record of every registration, session, meeting, and exhibitor interaction across an entire event portfolio, feeding personalization and portfolio-level reporting. Claims "up to 40% more pipeline," "13% cost savings by consolidating tools." Exhibitor Activation module: partners manage their presence, track leads, measure success inside the organizer's portal. Notable 2026 move: **native MCP server with OAuth** so AI agents can query live event data. Pricing: fully quote-based; SpendHound benchmarks avg SMB ~$6K/yr, enterprise ~$171K/yr.

**Lesson:** the persistent cross-event profile is the "portfolio memory" pattern — Bright's equivalent is machine/campaign performance history across shows and venues, which compounds into expected-performance benchmarks nobody else can offer.

## Glisser — cautionary footnote

Glisser (audience engagement/hybrid platform, UK/US, founded 2014) went into **administration in February 2023**; the platform sold but the business didn't survive the post-pandemic virtual-events contraction (micebook, The Gazette notice, Exhibition News). Lesson: single-feature engagement tools without a revenue/ROI story for organizers got wiped out when budgets tightened. Bright's tie to *sponsorship revenue and measurable leads* is the moat Glisser lacked.

# HALF B — RESELLER / PARTNER ENABLEMENT PATTERNS

## Impartner (PRM) — co-branded collateral + deal registration

**URLs:** impartner.com/partner-relationship-management, impartner.com/co-branded-collateral, impartner.com/lead-management

- **Co-Branded Collateral module:** vendor uploads a PDF, draws logo/text boundary boxes in a template editor; partner uploads logo + contact info and the system **auto-generates co-branded artwork in minutes** (datasheets, brochures, leave-behinds, web banners). "AutoSense" auto-detects images/text to make assets brandable without design services. Partners filter the Asset Library for "co-brandable" items and save multiple versions. Explicitly targeted at "partners who do not have marketing teams of their own" — i.e., a trade-show sales rep.
- **Deal registration:** native forms sync real-time to vendor CRM to resolve channel conflict; leads expire/reclaim/reassign automatically if a partner doesn't act; revenue ties back to the originating partner for provable channel ROI. Claims: 296% ROI, 50% more partner-sourced deals.

## PartnerStack — commissions + partner marketplace

**URLs:** partnerstack.com, partnerstack.com/platform/partner-commissions

- Manages affiliate/referral/reseller motions in one place; **116K+ active partner marketplace** where vendors list programs and partners apply.
- **Commission engine is the heart:** flat, percentage, tiered, milestone, and **recurring rev-share / drip (installment) payouts**; partners get a branded portal showing assets, performance, and — critically — **transparent, on-time payout visibility** ("commissions automatically available for partners to withdraw"; vendor pays one monthly invoice).
- Lesson for Bright: *rev-share visibility is a retention feature.* An organizer or venue that can see accruing commission in real time keeps selling/hosting.

## Crossbeam — ecosystem data as sales intelligence

**URL:** crossbeam.com

- Account mapping: partners connect CRMs, see overlapping customers/prospects/open opps without exposing raw data. "Partner Score" cards quantify: "Your win rate with [partner]'s customers is 65% — 20% higher than average; average opp size 23% larger."
- Pattern: **quantified partner-sourced performance signals delivered inside the seller's workflow** (CRM widgets, MCP server for AI agents). For Bright: show organizers which of *their* exhibitors have activated Bright machines at other shows — instant warm-lead lists.

## Deal registration flow (cross-vendor best practice)

**Sources:** level6.com 7-step guide, magentrix.com, fullcast.com, channelscaler.com

Canonical flow: partner submits short form (**<10 fields**, dropdowns) → automated duplicate/conflict check → vendor approves within a **published 24–72h SLA** (auto-escalation as deadline nears) → approval grants **time-bound exclusivity (30–180 days)** + margin uplift → status visible to partner throughout ("partner never has to ask 'is my deal approved yet?'") → expiry rules prevent hoarding; renewals require documented progress. "Reverse deal registration": vendor pushes qualified leads *to* partners as pre-filled deal shells. Golden rule: once approved, **never let the direct team poach it** — trust is the currency.

## White-label microsites & through-channel marketing (TCMA)

**Sources:** zinfi.com (partner portal + Microsite & Landing Page Management), mindmatrix.net, unifyr.com

- Vendors provide pre-approved, brand-locked microsite/landing-page templates; partners customize logo/contact/local messaging via constrained fields; pages deploy **under the partner's own domain**; lead-capture forms **route data back to the vendor CRM with partner attribution** automatically.
- ZINFI specifically offers **co-branded event landing pages + registration forms partners publish independently**, with leads flowing back centrally. Claim: structured TCMA partners generate 3–5x more qualified leads than ad-hoc ones.
- Core design tension handled well: vendor locks brand elements (colors, fonts, key messaging); partner freely edits only designated zones.

## Speed-to-quote / partner CPQ

**Sources:** channelscaler.com/platforms/pricing-self-service-quoting-software, xait.com, mercura.io/features/dealer-platform, cincom.com

- Pattern: extend the vendor's pricing engine into a **self-service partner portal** — guided configuration, dealer-specific price lists, approved discount bands, auto-generated **branded, customer-ready proposals**, 24/7 without emailing HQ. Mercura ships a **Frontend Form SDK to embed the whole configure-price-quote flow into the partner's own portal/site**.
- Outcome language across vendors: "from inquiry to quote in minutes rather than days"; "dealers produce accurate, branded quotes in minutes — instead of waiting days for HQ."
- This is the #1 determinant of whether resellers actually sell: if the organizer's rep can price a Bright machine live in the sponsor meeting, the deal doesn't die in email limbo.

## Embedded ROI calculators as sales tools

**Sources:** studionope.com ROI Calculator Pro, outgrow.co (15 B2B calculator teardowns)

- Best practice: chained formulas (visitors → leads → revenue → ROI) with sliders; results animate; **state encodes in the URL** so "a sales rep can configure the calculator with a prospect's actual numbers, copy the link, and paste it into an email. The prospect… lands on the page with their numbers already loaded." Lead-gating on CTA click feeds CRM.
- HubSpot's own calculator pattern: frame outputs as "what's possible at the median for your peer group" — benchmark framing feels like competitive intelligence, not a vendor promise. Directly applicable to Bright's expected plays/leads-per-day numbers.

## SponsorUnited & SponsorPitch — sponsorship valuation and marketplaces

**URLs:** sponsorunited.com/platform, sponsorpitch.com/how-it-works

- **SponsorUnited SPND:** "Zillow for sponsorship" — AI pricing estimates trained on $13.3B+ in actual deals; per-asset/per-entitlement price benchmarks; **Proposal Evaluator** (upload a sponsorship proposal → AI returns audience/market fit, competitive landscape, pricing benchmarks, negotiation guide). Platform is explicitly four-sided: brands / rights holders / agencies / media — each gets a tailored pitch on one homepage.
- **SponsorPitch:** two-sided marketplace — properties post pitches on a "Pitch Board," brands filter by category/market/budget; 13K+ sponsor database with deal history and decision-maker contacts; built-in CRM. Pricing tiers gate contact unlocks.
- Lesson: sponsorship buyers increasingly expect **benchmarked, data-justified pricing**. Bright's price bands + measured performance history are the raw material for a defensible SPND-style "expected value per activation" number.

## Media kit builders — live one-pagers beat PDFs

**Sources:** creatorsjet.com/media-kit, pop17.com media kit builder, usesheer.com

- Pattern shift: static PDF → **live hosted link with auto-updating stats** ("Replace screenshots and PDFs with one live link brands can review, forward, and approve on any device"; "stats update from live platform connections"). Kits package: proof (past partners, case studies), audience data, **rates and packages, and a booking CTA** in one scroll. Rationale: "PDFs stop improving the moment you attach them."
- Direct blueprint for Bright's sponsor pitch links: live page, auto-fed by real machine telemetry from comparable activations, forwardable inside the sponsor's org.

## Sponsorship tiering & prospectus mechanics

**Sources:** i4a.com, converve.com, claritymediapartners.com, eventbreakeven.com, loopyah.com

- 3–4 tiers max (choice paralysis beyond that); **geometric price spacing (1:2:4:8)**; top tier limited to 1–2 sponsors (scarcity protects value); top tier ~3–4x bottom tier.
- Tiers must ladder **access, not logo size**: Visibility → Engagement → Immersion/data rights (opt-in leads, immersive branded experiences at top tier — *this is where a Bright machine naturally sits*).
- Fixed tiers + à-la-carte add-on menu ("packages + add-ons, not one-off freebies") = customization without bespoke negotiation.
- Prospectus contents: event story, audience profile with buying authority, historical data + testimonials, **one-page tier comparison matrix, à-la-carte price menu, and a sample ROI report showing what sponsors will receive after the event**. Value-based pricing: sum the marketing-equivalent cost of bundled assets, price 30–50% below. Portfolio pricing (15–25% off multi-event commitments) framed as partnership, not discount.

## Sponsorship marketplaces & sales tooling

**Sources:** sponsormyevent.com, experialabs.com/platform/sponsorships, sponsorflo.ai, sirp.ai

- **SponsorMyEvent:** free listing, digital prospectus per event, in-platform negotiation/contracts/payments, **15% transaction fee** — a take-rate marketplace model.
- **SponsorFlo:** AI generates a fully branded sponsorship pitch deck **in 60 seconds** — cover, audience, package tiers, **photorealistic asset mockups**, pricing — from an asset library + prospect profile; slide-level engagement tracking on sent decks; follow-up automation.
- **Experia Creator OS:** sponsor deck builder (15 min), pipeline stages (Prospecting → Outreach → Proposal → Negotiation → Closed) with deal-value roll-up, **deliverables checklist ticked off as ops delivers ("sponsor sees the proof")**, one-click post-event ROI PDF, and a **per-sponsor portal** ("reduces 80% of 'any update?' emails").
- **SIRP AI:** audience-brand match scoring, activation tracking, automated post-event reports "that keep sponsors active."

## Proof-of-performance reporting norms

**Sources:** claritymediapartners.com (sponsor value demonstration system), portma.com, makaiinc.com

- Deliver within **48–72h of event close** (Bright's 24h beats the norm — say so).
- Structure: executive summary tied to the sponsor's stated objectives → objective-achievement scorecard → metric detail (financial impact / brand equity / relationship depth / strategic alignment) → **competitive context vs category benchmarks** → strategic recommendations for next time (the renewal setup).
- CMO-grade dashboards: qualified attendees (not raw traffic), engaged participants, leads meeting a quality bar, cost per qualified attendee, pipeline influenced. "The best dashboard tells a simple story in under two minutes."

## Venue-side revenue-share transparency (machine-placement world)

**Sources:** vendric.io, gzkwan.com, marwey.com, jvl.ca, pixelpay-arcade.com

- Placement economics: venue share typically **15–40% of gross** depending on venue type (malls 30–40%, supermarkets 15–25%); hybrid "minimum guarantee + % above threshold" is preferred by premium venues; contracts hinge on **audit rights and telemetry-verified revenue**.
- Tooling pattern: operators run fleet dashboards (per-machine P&L, health, telemetry) and give **location owners their own portal** to view commission history and payout PDFs — "Commission day used to take me 4 hours in Excel. Now I click one button and the PDF is in the location owner's inbox" (Vendric). Location portals use non-guessable UUID URLs — lightweight, no-login-friction sharing.
- Lesson: venue trust = telemetry-backed revenue statements they can self-serve, plus guaranteed-minimum options.

## Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Every winning platform tells a "one connected system" story on the homepage** — Swapcard ("From fragmented tools to a single revenue system," 7 named modules), Bizzabo ("Event Experience OS"), RainFocus (Global Attendee Profile), MYS ("The Growth Engine"). The modules are named, card-ified, and each side of the market gets a role-based section ("built for every role on your team").
2. **Proof-first homepages:** named flagship customers + hard numbers above the fold (ExpoPlatform: "50,000+ meetings at Shoptalk"; Swapcard: "€180K avg marketplace revenue"; Zenus: "16/16 booth renewals").
3. **ROI data is packaged as a product for the organizer's sales team**, not just a report for the sponsor: Grip Pulse ROI Predictor + at-risk detection, MYS Insights churn prediction, Zenus benchmark reports "so sponsorship sales personnel can tailor discussions." Measurement sells the *next* deal.
4. **Self-serve commerce inside the exhibitor/sponsor portal** is the highest-leverage monetization pattern: Swapcard Exhibitor Marketplace (+15–30% ARPE), A2Z Sponsorship Gallery + single cart, MYS sponsorships embedded in the booth application. Scarcity ("2 left"), live pricing, filter-by-budget.
5. **Shareable pre-loaded links close deals:** A2Z Sponsorship Link Builder (item dropped into cart), ROI calculators with URL-encoded state, live media-kit links, SponsorFlo tracked decks. The rep's job reduces to "send the link in the meeting."
6. **Reseller motion = speed + protection + transparency:** sub-10-field deal registration with 24–72h SLA and exclusivity; self-serve CPQ producing branded quotes in minutes; real-time rev-share/commission visibility; co-branded collateral generated in clicks without designers.
7. **Tiering physics:** 3–4 tiers, geometric pricing, exclusivity at the top, add-on menus, and tiers laddering *access and data* rather than logo size. Immersive branded experiences (Bright's category) belong at the top tier.
8. **Benchmarks are the new brochure:** SPND price estimates, Grip anonymized industry benchmarks, HubSpot "peer median" calculator framing. Buyers expect expected-performance ranges, not adjectives.

## What they do better than Bright.Experience (today)

- **Multi-sided storytelling in one screen.** Swapcard/Bizzabo name their modules and audiences on the homepage; Zenus splits organizers/exhibitors/brands into three doors. Bright's brand promise ("Crowd-stopping activations. Measured to the play.") covers the brand side but the organizer/venue sides of the platform are not yet a public, named story.
- **Self-serve sponsorship commerce.** A2Z/MYS/Swapcard let a sponsor go from browse → cart → contract → payment without a human. Bright's quote intake → proposal flow is consultative-only.
- **Predictive, benchmarked ROI.** Grip predicts exhibitor ROI *before* the show and benchmarks against anonymized peers; SponsorUnited prices deals off $13B of comparables. Bright has telemetry and price bands but (publicly) no expected-performance benchmark product.
- **Partner-grade tooling.** Impartner/ZINFI-class co-branded asset generation, deal registration with SLAs, embeddable partner CPQ, and payout dashboards are mature categories; nothing indicates Bright's organizer portal has these yet.
- **Marketplace network effects.** Cvent CSN monetizes venue exposure; PartnerStack's 116K-partner network recruits sellers. Bright's venue/organizer network isn't yet productized as discoverable supply.

## Gaps Bright could fill (whitespace nobody owns)

1. **Guaranteed-performance sponsorship inventory.** Nobody sells sponsorship units with telemetry-backed expected plays/leads *and* a performance floor. Zenus measures passively; Grip predicts from networking data; only Bright controls the physical activation end-to-end and can underwrite "X plays or credit."
2. **The activation layer as a category.** Event platforms manage *meetings and content*; Zenus measures *sentiment*; no platform owns "measurable physical brand experiences" as reusable, bookable, benchmarked inventory across shows AND venues AND retail. Bright can be the RainFocus-style "global activation profile": every machine placement compounds the benchmark dataset.
3. **Organizer sales enablement for experiential inventory.** SponsorFlo/Experia serve organizers selling *their own* inventory; PRMs serve software channels. Nobody gives a trade-show sales team a co-branded, expected-performance-backed toolkit for reselling a third party's physical activation. That's Bright's organizer portal, one level deeper.
4. **Venue-side yield marketplace.** Cvent CSN proves venues will maintain profiles to win RFPs; vending tools prove location owners want commission dashboards. A "host a Bright machine" supply marketplace with transparent expected revenue per placement doesn't exist.

## Implementation ideas for Bright (specific portal/page/feature)

**Organizer portal — "sell a Bright sponsorship in one meeting":**
1. **Pitch Link Builder (A2Z pattern + media-kit pattern).** Per show slot: a live, co-branded, no-login page (non-guessable URL, Vendric-style) showing machine renders/video, the sponsor's category exclusivity status, **expected-performance band** (plays/day, leads/day, dwell — drawn from Bright's telemetry benchmarks for comparable shows), tier pricing, and a "Reserve this slot" CTA that opens deal registration. URL state pre-loads the sponsor's name/vertical so the page reads as *their* proposal. Track opens/dwell per link (SponsorFlo pattern) and surface "sponsor viewed your pitch 3x" to the rep.
2. **Embedded ROI calculator on every pitch link.** Sliders: expected attendees, target audience %, show days → outputs leads, cost-per-qualified-lead vs the show's CPM-equivalent benchmarks ("peer median" framing). Rep can set values live in the meeting; link preserves state.
3. **Deal registration + slot holds.** ≤8-field form (sponsor, tier, machine config, target show), instant duplicate/exclusivity check against the show's slot inventory, **24h approval SLA** with auto-escalation, 14-day exclusivity hold shown as a countdown on the readiness board. Reverse-registration: Bright pushes inbound brand leads for that show to the organizer as pre-filled deal shells.
